You're right that every ALE figure rests on estimates, not measurements: the Annualized Rate of Occurrence and the Exposure Factor are informed judgments, not observed constants, because we rarely have ten years of clean incident history for a single company to calibrate them precisely. What is not made up is the arithmetic and the inputs we do have hard numbers for, like the 180,000-euro payment amount, the 8,000-euro website asset value, or last year's actual four-hour outage, which anchor the softer estimates to something real. The model's promise was never "this is exactly what ransomware will cost us this year"; its promise is that if the same negotiated inputs are used every time, two risks become comparable on the same footing, and a decision made today can be re-derived and challenged by someone else next year. That auditability is precisely what a red-amber-green cell cannot offer, since two people can agree a risk is "High" while meaning completely different things by it, and no one can trace the number back to its assumptions. So the honest position is: the estimate is a documented, defensible judgment, not a fact, and its value is consistency and traceability rather than precision. A defensible 8,000 euros beats "High" not because it is more accurate, but because everyone in the room can see exactly what it assumes and argue about the right thing.
