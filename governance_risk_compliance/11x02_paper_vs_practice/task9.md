CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001 answer two different questions, not the same one. CSF 2.0 is a voluntary framework of outcomes — it tells Tessara what "good" looks like across six functions and lets us measure our own current-versus-target state, but nobody outside the company can attest to it, because there is no independent audit body that certifies a CSF profile. ISO/IEC 27001 is the opposite: it is a certifiable standard, meaning an accredited third party formally examines Tessara's ISMS against Annex A controls and issues a certificate that a procurement team can independently verify — which is precisely what cost us the nine-hundred-thousand-euro deal, because "we follow CSF" is a self-assessment claim a buyer's security team cannot check. That is the commercial gap only ISO can close: it converts an internal claim into third-party proof. Our roadmap therefore does not choose between them; it sequences them, because CSF drives the actual improvement work now — closing gaps like the missing risk register and undocumented offboarding — before we pay a certification body to audit something worth certifying. Certifying an ISMS full of paper compliance would only convert our internal contradictions into an external, five-figure-priced audit finding.
