# 4 — Scope Memo: Latchkey's assessment boundary

This memo assigns the specific observed items to the party that can change or authorise them. “Not verified” is a finding about evidence, not a third ownership category: for account settings and data classification, Copperline must obtain the evidence or remediate the configuration. Latchkey's assessment is limited to API results supplied by the disposable lab session; it did not inspect AWS physical infrastructure or Copperline hosts.

## Copperline-controlled account configuration and decisions

| Observed item | Responsible side and why | Assessment consequence |
|---|---|---|
| IAM user `svc-audit-intake`, its active access-key record, inline policy `intake-ops-access`, attached `CopperlineOpsLogsReadOnly`, membership in `migration-readers`, and inherited `MigrationReadOnly` | **Copperline.** These are account identity, credential, and authorization objects. Copperline administrators can issue/revoke keys, change user/group attachments, and edit account policies. | Review the service identity's business need and key lifecycle. The one active key metadata record is not proof of key misuse. |
| Original `CopperlineOpsLogsReadOnly` v1 wildcard statement (`s3:Get*`/`s3:List*`, `Resource: "*"`) and remediation to v2 | **Copperline.** A managed policy is still Copperline-authored account configuration. The account API accepted the version change; v2 now has only `ReadOpsLogs` scoped to the ops-logs bucket and objects. | The unintended broad grant is remediated for this policy. Post-change checks showed ops-log listing success and scratch listing denial. Copperline should retain the change record and monitor for policy-version changes. |
| Inline `MaintainIntakePolicy` permission to create, activate, and delete versions of `CopperlineOpsLogsReadOnly` | **Copperline.** It is an explicit statement in the user's inline policy and was sufficient for the successful version creation/default change. | Independent backups, cross-account copies, recovery testing, data retention/classification, workloads and dependencies | **Copperline.** These are architecture and business decisions or customer-managed code/configuration. | Not established by the control-plane calls. ## Provider-operated platform boundary

| Item outside Latchkey's account-level test authority | Responsible side and why | Appropriate evidence or escalation |
|---|---|---|
| Physical security and operation of AWS Ireland data centres, hardware/media handling, and isolation between provider tenants | **Cloud provider.** Copperline and Latchkey cannot configure or directly inspect these controls through this IAM identity; attempting infrastructure-level tests would be outside this review's authority. | Use current provider assurance reports, certifications, contractual commitments, and provider support. Do not report an untested provider mechanism as a Copperline misconfiguration. |
| Patching and security of underlying hosts/control-plane infrastructure that operates S3, SQS, Secrets Manager, and KMS | **Cloud provider for the managed-service layer.** Copperline cannot patch the provider's service hosts. Copperline still owns its IAM permissions, resource policies, encryption choices, logging, and application use of the services. | Refer service-side defects or unexplained persistent API inconsistencies through provider support and assurance channels. Keep account-side configuration findings with Copperline. |
| Durability mechanisms and key hardware inside the managed platform | **Cloud provider for platform implementation; Copperline for its recovery design and key/data choices.** | Provider durability claims belong in provider assurance review. Whether Copperline needs independent copies, retention, restore tests, or customer-managed keys remains Copperline's decision and is not established by provider operation. |

The boundary is applied to each control: Copperline owns the account objects, permissions, data and configuration listed above; the provider owns the physical and managed-service machinery. Provider operation does not establish that Copperline's chosen access, retention, or recovery settings meet its needs. Conversely, this assessment makes no unsupported claim that the provider's internal controls failed.
