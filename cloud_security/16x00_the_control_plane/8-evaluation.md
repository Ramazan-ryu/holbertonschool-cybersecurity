# 8 — Review: The Label and the Evaluation

The managed policy is named `CopperlineOpsLogsReadOnly`, which suggests read access to operations logs. Its `ReadOpsLogs` statement is scoped to `copperline-ops-logs-cl7f3a`. The gap is the separate statement with `"Sid": "IntakeSupport"`: it allows `"Action": ["s3:Get*", "s3:List*"]` and sets `"Resource": "*"` in `evidence/iam-get-policy-version.json`. That resource scope is every resource to which those S3 actions can apply, not just the operations-log bucket. The account's successful listing of the undocumented migration-scratch bucket and its keys, and successful `HeadObject` responses for those keys, demonstrate that the broader scope has practical effect. The zero-byte `HeadObject` results conflict with the earlier listed sizes, so they do not establish what the object bodies contain.

This is a plausible deadline-shaped mistake. The team was moving dispatch operations on a four-month schedule, and an intake/support role can appear to need read access across several buckets while migration artifacts are being located. A broad action wildcard and `Resource: "*"` make that request easy to express and keep working as new buckets appear. The policy name and the narrow `ReadOpsLogs` statement can then make a quick review feel complete, even though IAM evaluates every statement and the second one independently expands access. The issue is the policy's construction and review path: the support exception was not constrained to named buckets or checked with calls as the actual principal. It is fixable by retaining the operations-log statement and removing the wildcard support statement, then proving the required log read still succeeds and the previously successful out-of-scope call is denied.
