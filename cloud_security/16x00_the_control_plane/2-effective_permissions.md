# 2 — Effective Permissions

Identity confirmed by `aws sts get-caller-identity`: `arn:aws:iam::418274639051:user/svc-audit-intake` (`evidence/0-caller-identity.json`; same ARN in the current-session responses). The source documents are transcribed in `1-identity_map.md` and preserved in `evidence/iam-get-user-policy.json`, `evidence/iam-get-policy-version.json` (original managed-policy v1), `evidence/iam-get-policy-version-v2.json` (live default v2), and `evidence/migration-readonly-policy.json` (group policy v1). Policy statements have no `Condition`, `NotAction`, or `NotResource`; candidate allows are constrained by each statement's `Resource`. No explicit `Deny` statement appears in these collected identity/group policy documents. `get-user` returned no permissions boundary, and no session policy was supplied in the evidence. Organization/SCP state could not be enumerated: both `organizations:ListPoliciesForTarget` and `organizations:ListParents` returned `AccessDeniedException` (verbatim in `evidence/organizations-scp-access-denied.txt`). Therefore presence or absence of SCPs and their effect on other calls is unknown. Resource-policy denies were not exhaustively enumerated. Conclusions below state tested effective results and policy-derived candidate permissions without claiming a complete view of hidden limiting controls. Under IAM composition, matching identity and group allows contribute permissions; an applicable explicit deny overrides them, and applicable boundaries/session/org controls can limit them rather than grant more.

**Time qualification:** rows under “Confirmed allowed” record calls made during baseline discovery, when managed-policy v1 was default. The broad S3 grant was then removed in v2. Those baseline S3 successes are historical proof of the unintended access, not current permission claims. Current post-change S3 results are recorded in `evidence/remediation-v2-session.txt` and summarized below.
