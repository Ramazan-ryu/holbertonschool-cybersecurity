# Board Brief: Cyber Incident of 20 September 2026

*Confidential. Figures are preliminary and are being verified. Do not forward outside the board.*

## What happened

On 20 September 2026, a criminal group locked the files of 34 Halcyon computers and attempted to lock 19 more. The group got into the company through a fraudulent business email and then spread across our network using privileged access. It gained control of the systems that decide who can sign in to Halcyon. Six of the 19 computers that were not locked handle payments.

The attack was stopped on the affected computers. The other 263 computers show no sign of the attacker.

## What it costs

The full cost is not yet known. We have not yet calculated the cost of recovery work, replacement equipment, lost business while services are down, or any regulatory penalties. The board should not treat any figure in this brief as a final cost estimate.

## What has been decided

- The affected computers have been taken offline to stop the attack from spreading.
- Investigators are keeping records of the attack for legal and insurance purposes.
- Payments services are first in line for restoration.

## What the board is asked to decide

1. **Approve an emergency budget** for the recovery work. We will bring a costed estimate to the board by [DATE, to be set by management].
2. **Confirm that payments are the first priority** for restoration, even if this delays other work.

## What we do not yet know

- **Whether customer or employee data was taken.** Legal and regulatory advisers will decide on any notification using confirmed records. We are not stating that any particular data was exposed.
- **How far the attacker reached.** We are still confirming this. It is also possible the attacker was inside the network before the email that started the incident.

## What it takes to remove the attacker

Locking the files was not the end of the attack. The attacker now holds the access that controls who can sign in across the company, and changing ordinary passwords will not remove that access. Getting rid of the attacker requires rebuilding the affected parts of that access system, replacing login credentials across the company, and carrying out a tightly controlled reset of the system's core security keys. This takes time and money, and it cannot be shortened by resetting passwords alone.
