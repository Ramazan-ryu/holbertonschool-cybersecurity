# Executive Brief

Halcyon Payments suffered a ransomware attack on 20 September 2026. The attacker encrypted 53 computers. Nineteen more computers, including six in payments processing, were under the attacker’s control but were stopped before their files were encrypted. The remaining 244 computers were not included in the confirmed attack set.

The attack began with a targeted purchase-order email carrying a harmful document. The attacker then moved through the company using an administrator account and reached the company’s central identity system. We have isolated the affected computers and preserved the evidence.

The hard truth is that stopping the encryption is not the same as removing the attacker. The attacker obtained a level of access that can let them return after ordinary password changes. Recovery therefore requires rebuilding affected systems, changing exposed credentials, checking both identity servers, and resetting the central ticket-signing secret twice under controlled conditions.

The clear Monday action is to approve emergency domain recovery and business-continuity funding, with payments protected first. Legal and regulatory teams should determine disclosure using confirmed identifiers and the impact assessment; this brief does not claim that unaffected records were exposed.
