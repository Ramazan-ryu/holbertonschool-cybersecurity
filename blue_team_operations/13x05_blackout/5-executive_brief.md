# Executive Brief: Ransomware Incident, 20 September 2026

## What happened

On 20 September 2026, a criminal group encrypted files on 53 Halcyon computers. The attack began with a targeted email containing a harmful document. The attacker then used a privileged administrator account to spread through the company and gained control of the central identity system, which manages access for every employee.

Nineteen more computers were under the attacker's control and were isolated before their files were encrypted. Six of them handle payments. The remaining 244 computers show no confirmed attacker activity.

## What it costs

The direct cost is not yet known. It includes recovery labour, rebuilt systems, lost business while payments and other services are restored, and any regulatory consequences. These figures need to be estimated before the budget is finalised.

## What has been decided

- The affected and at-risk computers have been isolated.
- Evidence has been preserved for investigation.
- Payments systems are being given priority in recovery.

## Decision required on Monday

Approve funding for emergency recovery of the central identity system and for business continuity, with payments first.

## What remains uncertain

- Whether any customer or employee data was accessed. Legal and regulatory teams will decide on disclosure using confirmed records. This brief does not claim that unaffected records were exposed.
- How far the attacker's control of the identity system extends.

## The hard truth

Stopping the encryption did not remove the attacker. They obtained control of the identity system, which can let them return even after ordinary password changes. Removing them requires rebuilding affected systems, replacing exposed credentials across the company, checking both identity servers, and resetting a central security secret twice under controlled conditions. This takes time and cannot be shortened by changing passwords alone.
