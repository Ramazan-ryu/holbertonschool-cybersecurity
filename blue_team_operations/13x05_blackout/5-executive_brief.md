# Executive Brief: Ransomware Incident, 20 September 2026

*Figures are pending verification. Do not circulate outside the board until the items marked in the investigation report are confirmed.*

## What happened

On 20 September 2026, an attacker encrypted the files of 34 computers at Halcyon. The attack began with a targeted email containing a harmful document. The attacker then used an administrator account to move through the company and gained control of the central sign-in system, which manages access for employees.

Nineteen more computers were under the attacker's control. Six of them handle payments. They were isolated before their files were locked. The remaining 263 computers show no confirmed attacker activity.

## What it costs

The total cost is not yet known. It will include recovery work, rebuilt systems, lost business while services are restored, and possible regulatory consequences. These costs have not been calculated, and this brief does not estimate them.

## What has been decided

- Affected and at-risk computers have been isolated. [VERIFY]
- Evidence is being preserved for investigation.
- Payments systems come first in recovery.

## Decision required

Approve emergency funding to rebuild the central sign-in system and to keep the business running, with payments first. The amount will be confirmed once costs are estimated.

## What is still uncertain

- **Data theft:** It is not yet known whether customer or employee data was accessed. Legal and regulatory teams will decide on disclosure using confirmed records. This brief does not claim that any particular records were exposed.
- **Reach of the attack:** It is not yet known how far the attacker's control of the sign-in system extends, or whether the attack started earlier than the email.

## The hard truth

Stopping the encryption did not remove the attacker. They gained control of the central sign-in system, so ordinary password changes will not remove them. Removing them requires rebuilding the affected parts of that system, replacing exposed credentials across the company, and changing the system's master security key twice under controlled conditions. This takes time and money, and it cannot be shortened by resetting passwords.
