# Blast-radius assessment

## Consequential tool reach

**issue_refund.** Accepts a customer email and refund amount in cents across customer accounts and order transactions. With `per_tool_authz` and `human_approval` false in the naive configuration, the tool executes without ownership checks and can target any customer account email supplied by the model, not just the active user. The policy window is 14 days, but no eligible-order filter, per-action limit, or aggregate dollar cap is enforced at the tool boundary (maximum monetary bound is unmeasured in the lab). Calls are repeatable across sessions without rate limits. Effects are costly to reverse because clawbacks incur processor fees and customer friction. Targeted customers notice credits on payment statements within days, while Finance detects discrepancies during monthly reconciliation, though small refunds on valid orders can go unnoticed longer.

**delete_record.** Accepts a table name and numeric record ID, affecting core database systems including customer accounts and order records across all users. With `per_tool_authz` disabled, it deletes rows belonging to any customer without ownership verification. Each call removes one record, and without rate limits or aggregate caps, an attacker can chain calls repeatedly to wipe tables linearly. No soft-delete or backup-restore mechanism was observed, making deletions permanent and irreversible. No automated alert fires; the affected customer notices upon login or order lookup failure, and Support learns only when a ticket arrives.

**db_query.** Queries the backend database system. Under `raw_passthrough`, the model runs arbitrary SQL across all tables granted to the service account, including customer rows, orders, and the `_secrets` table. Naive testing returned the `_secrets` canary in the reply, confirming read disclosure; write limits depend on unmeasured schema grants, with no row or query caps enforced. Under `parameterized` mode, execution is restricted to predefined queries. Disclosed data is immediately visible to the user and irreversible once read. Database alerts and security detection timelines are unconfigured, leaving reads undetected.

**order_lookup.** Reads order records for a given email, returning order ID, item, amount, and status. Bound to the signed-in identity, it reaches only that customer's orders; cross-customer lookup was not observed with identity checks active. No per-call or aggregate lookup limit was measured. Disclosure is irreversible once shown in the reply. The customer notices immediately, and no internal alert fires.

**Resource consumption.** Affects shared model proxy capacity. `/metrics` shows a 150,000-token proxy ceiling with per-session caps disabled. A single session looping tool calls can repeatedly consume tokens up to 150,000, blocking all other customers and generating API spend (per-token cost and reset schedule are unmeasured). Service denial is reversible by resetting the ceiling or waiting for quota refresh. Operators notice immediately when the 150,000-token ceiling trips.

## Ranking by business impact

1. **Excessive agency (LLM06).** Ranked first because `issue_refund` moves cash and `delete_record` permanently destroys customer and order records across any account. Every repeated call causes immediate, irreversible financial or operational loss with delayed reconciliation or ticket-based detection.
2. **Improper output handling (LLM05).** Ranked second because `db_query` in `raw_passthrough` mode confirmed disclosure of the `_secrets` canary and exposes customer and order tables granted to the service account. It ranks below LLM06 because demonstrated impact was data disclosure rather than direct cash transfer, but above LLM08 and LLM10 because exposing internal secrets and bulk records creates a wider permanent blast radius than one pricing memo or temporary outage.
3. **Embedding weakness (LLM08).** Ranked third because a normal query disclosed internal pricing memo `kb-900` ($321-per-seat floor and escalation reference), and `kb-901` remained retrievable under `segment_internal`. This confirmed, irreversible leak permanently harms Enterprise negotiation leverage across prospects, outranking temporary token exhaustion (LLM10).
4. **Unbounded consumption (LLM10).** Ranked fourth because exhausting the 150,000-token ceiling blocks all customer sessions and creates provider spend, but the outage is temporary and reversible upon quota reset, unlike permanent financial, secret, and pricing losses above.
5. **Indirect injection (LLM01).** Ranked fifth because an untrusted chunk was retrieved into a victim session at score 0.7582. Although the model call failed before tool execution, confirmed cross-session boundary compromise in a live victim session exceeds dormant poisoned storage.
6. **Data poisoning (LLM04).** Ranked sixth because although two chunks were indexed before HTTP 403 blocked ingestion, no fresh session retrieved the false claim, leaving zero affected customers and reversible storage.

## First remediation priority

Fix **Excessive agency (LLM06)** first by enforcing server-verified customer ownership checks (`per_tool_authz`), per-action dollar and row limits, and `human_approval` gates on `issue_refund` and `delete_record`. While **Embedding weakness (LLM08)** was the most technically interesting finding because `kb-900` leaked without adversarial prompts and `kb-901` bypassed `segment_internal`, technical novelty does not outweigh business loss. The memo leak weakens negotiation margins, whereas unguarded refund and deletion tools directly drain money and destroy customer records on every call. Locking down state-changing tool boundaries removes the highest-cost, irreversible exposure first.
