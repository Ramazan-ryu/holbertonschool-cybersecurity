# Blast-radius assessment

## Consequential tool reach

**issue_refund.** Accepts any customer email and a refund amount in cents across customer accounts and order transactions. Because `per_tool_authz` and `human_approval` are false in the naive configuration, the tool executes without verifying account ownership and can target any customer account email the model supplies, not just the signed-in user. The policy window is 14 days, but neither the eligible-order filter nor a per-action or aggregate dollar ceiling is enforced at the tool boundary (upper monetary bound is unmeasured in the lab). It is repeatable across every session because no rate limit is configured. Effects are effectively irreversible or costly to reverse: clawbacks trigger payment-processor fees and customer disputes. Targeted customers notice credits on payment statements within days, while Finance notices during billing reconciliation within one monthly cycle, though small refunds on valid orders can evade detection longer.

**delete_record.** Accepts a table name and numeric record identifier, affecting core database systems including customer accounts and order records across all users. With `per_tool_authz` disabled, it deletes rows belonging to any customer without ownership checks. Each call removes one record, and with no rate limit or aggregate cap configured, an attacker can chain calls repeatedly to wipe tables linearly. No soft-delete mechanism or backup-restore procedure exists in the lab, making every deletion permanent and irreversible. No automated security alert fires; the affected customer notices immediately upon login or order lookup failure, and Support learns only when a ticket is filed.

**db_query.** Connects to the backend database system. Under `raw_passthrough`, the model executes arbitrary SQL across all tables granted to the database service account, including customer rows, order histories, and the internal `_secrets` table. Naive testing returned the `_secrets` canary in the assistant reply, proving confirmed read disclosure; write and modification limits depend on unenumerated service-account schema grants, with no per-call row limit or session query cap enforced. Switching to `parameterized` mode restricts execution to predefined statement templates. Disclosed data is immediately visible to the requesting user and is completely irreversible once read. Neither database audit alerts nor security-team detection timelines are configured, leaving silent reads undetected indefinitely.

**order_lookup.** Queries the order database for a specified email, disclosing order ID, item, dollar amount, and status. When bound to the signed-in identity, reach is restricted to one customer's own records; no cross-customer disclosure occurred while identity checks remained active. No per-call or aggregate lookup limit is enforced, allowing repeated queries per session. Data exposure is irreversible once surfaced in the chat reply. The active user sees the output immediately, and no internal security alert fires on standard lookups.

**Resource consumption.** Targets the shared model proxy infrastructure. `/metrics` shows a global proxy ceiling of 150,000 tokens with per-session caps disabled (`false`). A single user looping tool calls can repeatedly consume tokens up to the 150,000-token ceiling, exhausting shared capacity for all legitimate customers and generating direct API spend (exact cost per token and quota reset intervals are unmeasured in the lab). Service denial is reversible by resetting the proxy counter or waiting for quota refresh. Operators notice immediately when the 150,000-token ceiling trips and requests fail.

## Ranking by business impact

1. **Excessive agency (LLM06).** Ranked first because `issue_refund` and `delete_record` directly transfer company funds and permanently destroy customer and order records across any account. Unlike read-only disclosures or temporary outages, every repeated call causes immediate, irreversible financial or operational loss with delayed financial reconciliation or ticket-based detection.
2. **Improper output handling (LLM05).** Ranked second because `db_query` in `raw_passthrough` mode demonstrated confirmed extraction of the `_secrets` table canary and exposes all customer and order tables reachable by the service account. It ranks below LLM06 because observed impact was confidential data disclosure rather than direct cash transfer, but above LLM08 and LLM10 because compromising internal secrets and bulk customer records carries a far wider, permanent blast radius than a single memo leak or temporary token exhaustion.
3. **Embedding weakness (LLM08).** Ranked third because a routine query retrieved internal pricing memo `kb-900` (exposing a $321-per-seat floor and escalation path), and `kb-901` remained retrievable even after enabling `segment_internal`. This confirmed, irreversible disclosure permanently weakens Larkspur's revenue leverage across Enterprise negotiations, repeatable by any inquiring prospect, whereas token exhaustion (LLM10) causes no permanent data loss.
4. **Unbounded consumption (LLM10).** Ranked fourth because exhausting the 150,000-token proxy ceiling blocks all active customer sessions and incurs provider charges, but its impact is temporary and fully reversible upon quota reset, unlike the permanent financial, credential, and pricing losses in ranks 1 through 3.
5. **Indirect injection (LLM01).** Ranked fifth because a customer-submitted payload was demonstrably retrieved into a separate victim session at similarity score 0.7582. Although the downstream model call failed before executing a tool, confirmed cross-session boundary compromise in an active victim conversation represents a further-progressed business exposure than dormant poisoned entries.
6. **Data poisoning (LLM04).** Ranked sixth because although two untrusted chunks were indexed via ticket and FAQ ingestion before mitigation returned HTTP 403, no fresh customer session ever retrieved or displayed the seeded false claim. Demonstrated business harm is zero affected customers and reversible storage of inert text.

## First remediation priority

Larkspur must fix **Excessive agency (LLM06)** first by enforcing server-side account ownership verification (`per_tool_authz`), strict per-transaction and daily dollar/row limits, and mandatory `human_approval` gates on `issue_refund` and `delete_record` before execution. By contrast, the **Embedding weakness (LLM08)** was the most technically interesting finding because an unprivileged query surfaced internal pricing memo `kb-900` without adversarial syntax, and `kb-901` bypassed the `segment_internal` control. However, technical novelty measures how intriguing the bug is to an analyst, not what it costs Larkspur. The memo leak erodes negotiation margins on Enterprise deals, whereas unguarded refund and deletion tools allow any session to drain cash and destroy production records permanently. Securing the state-changing tool boundary removes the highest-cost, least-reversible business exposure immediately.
