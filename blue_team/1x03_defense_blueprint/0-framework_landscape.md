# Task 0: The Framework Landscape

## Part 1 - Three-Framework Summary

### NIST CSF 2.0
- **What it is and who publishes it**: The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 is published by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). It provides a comprehensive framework for organizations to manage and reduce cybersecurity risk.

- **What it is designed to do**: The primary purpose of the NIST CSF is to assist organizations in identifying, assessing, and managing cybersecurity risks to ensure their critical infrastructure is protected.

- **How it is structured**: The framework is structured into five core functions: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover. Each function contains categories and subcategories that outline specific outcomes.

- **Who typically uses it and in what context**: NIST CSF is utilized by diverse organizations, including healthcare, finance, and government, particularly those striving to enhance their cybersecurity posture and align with regulatory requirements.

### CIS Controls v8
- **What it is and who publishes it**: The CIS Controls v8 is published by the Center for Internet Security (CIS) and comprises a set of best practices designed to improve an organization’s cyber defense.

- **What it is designed to do**: It provides organizations with a prioritized list of actions to mitigate the most common cyber threats and vulnerabilities, acting as a guide to implementing effective cybersecurity measures.

- **How it is structured**: The controls are organized into three Implementation Groups (IG1, IG2, IG3) based on the maturity of the organization's security environment and the complexity of threats they face.

- **Who typically uses it and in what context**: These controls are widely adopted by organizations of all sizes, especially those looking for practical and prioritized cybersecurity implementations.

### ISO 27001
- **What it is and who publishes it**: ISO/IEC 27001 is an international standard for managing information security, published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO).

- **What it is designed to do**: Its goal is to help organizations establish, implement, maintain, and continuously improve an Information Security Management System (ISMS) to protect sensitive information.

- **How it is structured**: The standard includes specific requirements for establishing an ISMS, risk management, and controls to mitigate risks.

- **Who typically uses it and in what context**: ISO 27001 is commonly used by organizations that handle sensitive information and need to demonstrate information security compliance to customers and regulators.

## Part 2 - Relationship Map
The NIST CSF, CIS Controls, and ISO 27001 are complementary frameworks rather than competitors. The NIST CSF provides strategic guidance on what organizations should do to manage cybersecurity risks, while CIS Controls outline specific steps on how to achieve those strategies effectively. ISO 27001 serves as a framework for demonstrating compliance with information security practices. Together, they create a comprehensive approach: NIST CSF answers "What should we do?", CIS Controls answer "How should we do it?", and ISO 27001 answers "Can we prove we are doing it?".

## Part 3 - MedDefense Framework Selection
MedDefense should adopt the NIST CSF as its strategic backbone while also implementing the CIS Controls for practical guidance. Given that MedDefense is a regional hospital with a small security team and the need for compliance, this combination will enable it to develop a robust cybersecurity strategy while providing specific measures to enhance its security posture. This will not only address immediate security needs but also align with regulatory expectations, creating a reliable foundation for ongoing cybersecurity practices.
