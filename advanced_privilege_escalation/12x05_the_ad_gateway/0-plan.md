
# Plan Against an Untrusted Graph

I will treat the supplied domain-user credentials as a starting point for a bounded assessment, not as permission to pursue every reachable object. The objective is to identify and validate the permission chain to the group that controls the production policy. Taking control of the domain controller, protected accounts, or unrelated systems is outside scope.

I will enumerate in this order. First, I will confirm the domain, available directory services, and the account’s identity and direct group memberships. Second, I will map users, groups, computers, organizational units, nested memberships, trusts, and relevant attributes using directory queries. Third, I will inspect access-control entries on objects that could connect the starting identity to the stated objective, including ownership, delegated rights, and inheritance. Fourth, I will identify linked Group Policy Objects and their SYSVOL contents, then perform only authorized, low-impact service discovery on in-scope hosts to understand where those policies apply. I will record timestamps and the exact query or tool output for each finding.

The model I am building is a current, evidence-backed map of principals and their relationships: who belongs to which groups, which objects each principal can read or modify, how those rights are inherited, which computers and OUs are in scope, and which policies apply to them. A graph collector can suggest connections, but its edges are leads, not proof; its data may be stale, incomplete, or misleading about effective access.

Before investing in a proposed edge, I will record its source, target, claimed right, and the directory evidence that should support it. I will query the relevant object’s current membership or security descriptor, resolve the trustee SID, and check the specific access mask, inheritance, and object scope. For a read edge, I can make a minimal read-only request with the starting credentials. For a write edge, I will not change the object just to prove the point: I will require the matching effective ACL and confirm that exercising it is explicitly authorized.

The failure I am guarding against is shortest-path fixation: forcing an attractive route because the graph draws it, even when the required right is absent or unusable. If an edge fails, I will record the claim, evidence, and failed test, mark the assumption unsupported, and reassess other evidence-backed leads rather than retrying the same primitive. I will stop at the production-policy group membership objective and will not pursue domain-controller control.
