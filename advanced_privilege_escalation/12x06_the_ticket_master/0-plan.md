# Plan Through the Authentication System

Thornbury’s database server is an identity and authorization objective, not a box to exploit locally. I will first map how the domain identifies users, which services accept their authentication, and which account or delegation relationship could lead to an administrative session on the database server. Every action will be tied to that identity path and recorded with its evidence.

I will rank recoverable material before spending time cracking it. A candidate receives priority when it belongs to a database administrator, a service account with an SPN, an account with useful group membership or delegation rights, or an identity that can authenticate directly to the objective server. I will also consider the material type, password strength, available wordlists, and whether the account is likely to be disabled or restricted. Low-value user accounts and weakly relevant hashes will be tested briefly and then deprioritized.

My stopping rule is to reassess each candidate after a fixed period, such as two hours of targeted dictionary, mask, and rule-based attempts. I will stop earlier if the candidate has no useful relationship to the database server; I will stop later only when evidence shows high access value and measurable cracking progress. This prevents “crack everything” from consuming the engagement.

Plaintext credentials will be validated against domain identity and access relationships before use. I will determine the account’s groups, SPNs, delegation settings, and permitted access to the database server, then request only the tickets or sessions required to prove the path. If I recover an NT hash or Kerberos ticket but not the plaintext password, I will assess whether authorized pass-the-hash, pass-the-ticket, or overpass-the-hash use can authenticate as that identity; the proof must be a successful authenticated session and verified privilege, not merely possession of material.

Before acting, I expect the domain controller to record LDAP binds and directory queries, Kerberos TGT requests as event 4768, service-ticket requests as 4769, and failed authentication attempts as 4625 where applicable. I will not conduct broad password spraying or repeated guesses against many accounts, because that could create lockouts, generate avoidable failures, and disrupt the authentication environment. [Microsoft Kerberos auditing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/security/threat-protection/auditing/basic-audit-kerberos-authentication-service) and [MITRE Kerberoasting](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1558/003/) will guide the monitoring assumptions.
